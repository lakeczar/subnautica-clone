## Writer's truth: establish this before writing clues

The settlements were not emptied by a monster, alien intelligence, or an evacuation into space. Their surviving inhabitants are still here, in ordinary human suspension beds beneath the drowned central settlement. What looks like the aftermath of an uncontrolled flood includes an intentional rescue operation. The player personally ordered its last, terrible step.

The protagonist, **Ren**, was a working marine access engineer, not the expedition's commander. Ren knew the gates, service passages, and pressure systems. When the emergency severed communications, that practical knowledge made them the person able to execute a collectively designed refuge plan. They closed a barrier while six maintenance workers were still beyond it. Waiting might have saved those six; it would also have exhausted the refuge's measured pressure margin. The available evidence supports the decision's urgency, but cannot prove the counterfactual. Those six died. The later revelation must not resurrect them or declare Ren blameless.

Ren subsequently entered suspension with the other survivors, designated as the first repair specialist to wake when exterior conditions became viable. Seven years and four months later, a damaged launch pod carried them upward from the refuge. Its deteriorated regulator caused an oxygen shortfall during emergency ascent. Ren's missing local autobiographical memories follow that injury and suspension recovery; nobody secretly erased them. Skills and older memories survive unevenly. Recovery is partial, not a succession of perfectly convenient cinematic downloads.

The ocean has no human-style collective mind. Its native filter communities move and concentrate dense mineral brines. Human extraction disturbed that geography, and engineers then mistook intermittent warning measurements for faulty instrumentation. The disaster is regional and physical. Understanding currents, sediment, animal refuges, and human routing makes its causes legible and supplies the means of repair.

## Fixed chronology and accounting

1. **Nine years before the disaster:** a 218-person expedition establishes island workshops, small offshore laboratories, and a larger submerged research settlement. Familiar interstellar suspension equipment arrives with them. Their next supply vessel is scheduled eleven years after the eventual disaster; there is no nearby rescue fleet.
2. **Two years before:** demand for concentrated brine minerals expands extraction along the basin's supporting sediment ridge. Surveys identify changing filter colonies, undermined slopes, and intermittent dense-water incursions. Teams disagree about whether these indicate a local maintenance problem or a connected basin instability.
3. **Disaster day, first hour:** a storm loads the weakened island slope. Its collapse destroys the long-range mast and breaches the brine saddle. Sediment damages power and intake infrastructure; dense, oxygen-poor water enters the settlement's seawater oxygen-extraction circuits. Twenty people die in the initial failures. This is neither infection nor a planet-wide extinction event.
4. **Following five hours:** 198 survivors consolidate into the deep refuge. Closed suspension beds consume much less oxygen than awake people. Flooding abandoned outer galleries equalizes pressure and redirects flow through surviving turbines; the beds remain dry and sealed. Six volunteers stay outside to make that diversion possible. Ren holds the final gate until its documented pressure threshold, then closes it while they are still working. The six do not return.
5. **Before suspension:** the remaining **192 survivors include Ren**. They preserve the six names and disagree openly about the decision. Ren volunteers for the first maintenance awakening. The refuge's instructions and access routes do not depend on Ren remembering a password. Its caretaker is a limited maintenance controller, not a deceptive conversational intelligence.
6. **Seven years, four months later:** repeated exterior measurements finally establish a safe upper-water route and sustainable oxygen supply. The controller dispatches Ren in the only service pod it can launch autonomously. Age-damaged ascent hardware produces the opening accident. The other **191 survivors remain suspended**. They have a stable reserve during the campaign; there is no hidden real-time countdown.
7. **Campaign present:** the player restores an upper living area, dependable power/air, and safe refuge access. Waking sleepers beforehand would strand them. Main-story completion initiates supervised revival; an epilogue confirms all 191 awaken. Repairing the island mast permits later rescue communication but cannot replace the immediate local solution.

## Gate topology: the historical constraint and present solution

The six worked in an enclosed, initially dry turbine-return gallery outside the refuge's rated bulkhead, not in the open sea. The return gate was their only still-rated human exit: the collapse had broken the exterior airlock and buried its access tunnel. They used interior work equipment, not pressure suits able to survive the flooded gallery. The map, crushed outer lock and contemporary maintenance report must establish this before the full gate recording resolves the event.

| State | Physical condition and available action | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical evacuation | Refuge batteries and air reserve are near their documented limit. Six workers finish the manual diversion in the return gallery. The return gate is still open; Ren can delay for seconds but cannot know the outcome. | Delay may let workers reach the gate, while the refuge's remaining margin falls. |
| Historical closure | Closing the rated gate seals the refuge. The connected flood sluice then fills the outer gallery, pressure-balances the damaged outer structure and establishes the surviving turbine flow. | Reopening the gate admits that water into the occupied refuge. The trapped workers cannot escape through the damaged exterior lock. Their deaths are irreversible. |
| Historical rejected alternative | A narrow hydraulic service pipe could equalize empty compartments, but isolating the active turbine diversion to use it would exhaust the refuge's reserve before the occupied galleries could be made safe. | The emergency record explicitly considers and rejects this option. It is neither an overlooked human tunnel nor proof that a few seconds' delay was impossible. |
| Present repair | The galleries are empty. The player brings independent temporary power/air, isolates the active diversion and slowly equalizes compartments through the service pipe before opening the human route. | New preparation removes the old emergency constraint. The hydraulic bypass is not a newly discovered escape passage that could have saved the workers. |

Represent this with a small authored machinery state graph, readable schematic and visible pressure/power indicators. Exterior approach routes in the world document reach the works; they do not bypass this internal gate boundary. The present procedure is available from the machinery interface and its schematic even if a personal journal was missed. This establishes causality without deciding whether Ren waited long enough.

## Four human perspectives

- **Ren, access engineer:** clipped procedural recordings conceal exhaustion, not secret villainy. “Close the return” is genuinely Ren's order. A later complete recording adds the pressure reading and attempted alternatives without changing those words. Present-day Ren can investigate and disagree with their earlier judgment.
- **Dr. Iona Vale, suspension physician:** designs the refuge plan and insists on informed consent. She records survival statistics because she fears sentiment will obscure risk. Her confident early estimates are explicitly revised after new measurements; her certainty is not omniscience.
- **Tavi Orr, maintenance diver:** loses a partner among the six. Tavi understands the engineering but believes Ren closed too soon. Their accusation is sincere and remains morally unresolved after the facts are known. Tavi subsequently helps protect Ren's bed; grief and care coexist.
- **Mara Sen, basin ecologist:** detects the physical chain early but initially underestimates how quickly it will propagate. Her journals combine fascination with self-reproach. She leaves field methods and annotated specimen observations, not a complete plot explanation in her first notebook.

Date and identify records: these people knew different things at different times. Never invent contradictions to postpone a reveal. Names and voices await the cast/art pass.

## Opening: playable, brief, and spoiler-safe

Target a short in-engine sequence, approximately ninety seconds before ordinary control, adjustable after testing. Black water, a damaged gauge, intermittent light, pressure alarm. Ren wakes restrained as the pod rolls; they release the restraint, close a leaking service latch, and trigger a reserve purge. The window clears onto a luminous shallow reef as the pod settles. An island silhouette and distant artificial ribs promise exploration. Do not show a spacecraft crash or falsely imply a downward trajectory.

The pod offers storage, basic crafting, and healing. Its operational checklist survives; its navigation history is physically damaged and recoverable later. A small recorded self-introduction establishes Ren's name without assigning a remembered personality. Initial agency is tangible: repair a refuge, inspect nearby life, choose which visible landmark to approach. No attack interrupts the first sanctuary.

## Intended discovery structure

These allocations total roughly ten hours as a design hypothesis, not measured content or a promise. Optional exploration sits beyond them. Travel, repeated harvesting, and long audio playback cannot fill missing dramatic work.

| Act | Target | Question and playable consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I — A place to breathe | 0–1.5 h | Where am I, and why is this pod damaged? Establish refuge; follow either a reef service route or island workshop lead. Learn a resource habitat and a current cue. |
| II — The absent expedition | 1.5–3.5 h | Did people flee, die, or deliberately abandon these sites? Investigate two regional leads in either order. Recover complementary fabrication and expedition capabilities. Discover that the pod came from below. |
| III — The voice at the gate | 3.5–5.5 h | Why do records accuse me? Reach the disrupted basin boundary and access works. Reconstruct the physical failure and identify Ren's order. Preparation and return routes become meaningful. |
| IV — A population unaccounted for | 5.5–7.5 h | What did the flooding actually protect? Reconcile casualty and transfer records with living suspension telemetry. The refuge entrance delivers the explicit survivor reveal. Repair priorities replace speculative investigation. |
| V — Make somewhere to wake | 7.5–10 h | Can I finish what we began without repeating its costs? Prepare habitation, route power and air, perform a protected deep expedition, and initiate supervised revival. End with accountable human contact and renewed wonder. |

## Regional guidance, fair ordering, and recovery

Leads describe an identifiable region and a distinguishing feature: “the island workshop above the split stack,” or “the intake station where animals avoid the lower channel.” These are template examples pending geography. A journal preserves the observation, the current question, and useful preparation, with an optional player-placed marker. It does not instantly reveal an undiscovered site's coordinates.

Store findings as facts with provenance, separating observation from inference. Present a recovered record immediately even out of order; do not lock it until a scripted act. C13 found early reveals survivors early and changes the active question to how to wake them. Subsequent evidence explains responsibility and cause. Equipment gates regulate physical access, never manufacture amnesia about a discovered fact.

Essential facts need environmental evidence plus a durable record: physical ascent hardware and telemetry; bed counts and living scans; gate geometry and pressure logs. Optional personal journals deepen interpretation but cannot block completion. The refuge controller can supply a concise factual recovery summary once reached, clearly distinguishing unknown details. Damaged record copies remain recoverable from local redundant equipment, with matching content and provenance; do not randomly relocate clues into inventory. No critical interpretation requires reading a single tiny prop or hearing audio without captions.

## Ending and implementation limits

The finale recombines established skills: read animal/current cues to cross a disturbed channel, bring an appropriate utility or inhabited vessel, collect known habitat-specific repair stock, stage independent air/power and operate the hydraulic bypass before reopening the human route. These can be authored state transitions with visible consequences; a simulated planet, fluid dynamics solver, or autonomous colony is unnecessary.

After pressure and habitation checks, the controller offers a medically safe default first awakening independently of C15 or other optional messages. The player can choose among medically cleared candidates; one supervised bed opens. Tavi or Iona speaks a short, context-aware scene. Communal stores and a restored food-production module support the population; the player does not gather 191 individual meals or build 191 rooms. A time cut and populated roster confirm all 191 survivors awaken; the 26 casualties remain dead. Ren has made a new consequential choice without recovering a perfectly innocent identity. A restored lookout offers daylight, wildlife, and human presence. Credits follow a resolved rescue; optional exploration remains available.

Keep unapproved scope contained: a few recorded voices, one or two finale characters with limited staging, reusable bed modules, local machinery states, and a short opening. Mobile-home interiors, exact vehicle requirements, survival severity, resource budgets, settlement scale, and platform performance remain integration decisions. The writer owns chronology and clue meaning; the coordinator must check every required capability and material against reachability, recovery, and the ten-hour playtest target.
