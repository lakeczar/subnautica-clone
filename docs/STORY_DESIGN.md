# Mystery and discovery — review copy without major spoilers

The owner wants to discover the central mystery and the protagonist's forgotten role through play. They delegate both truths to the narrative team, ask for ambiguous early lore and a somewhat shocking reveal, and select regional leads. Neither the living-network suggestion nor the rescue-decision suggestion was selected as canon.

## What the player should experience

Wake inside a damaged refuge pod with missing memories. A short opening sequence establishes damage, disorientation and a nearby recoverable problem; it must not explain the hidden truth. Let the player act quickly: stabilize their refuge, look outside, recognize a landmark and choose a first investigation. Keep the opening grounded in the eventual true chronology.

Wonder leads. The ocean and islands are beautiful, inhabited by recognizable life and dotted with traces of people. Ruins raise specific questions rather than serving as interchangeable loot rooms. Deeper expeditions build tension; the pod and later inhabited vessels provide comfort and a place to reflect, prepare and arrange discoveries.

The central questions concern what happened to the settlements, who the player was and why they cannot remember. Early evidence supports more than one explanation. Discoveries gradually make some explanations less plausible; the major reveal changes the meaning of things the player has already seen. The ending must answer the central factual questions, while leaving room to interpret motives and consequences.

These are authored treatment choices under the user's delegated brief, not claims about an implemented or playtested story.

## Truth, ambiguity and evidence

- Write one canonical chronology before final scene dialogue or scan prose. Track who knew each fact, when they knew it, what they believed and why their account may be incomplete. An unreliable character is not permission for an inconsistent world.
- Seed the core reveal in at least three independent earlier clues, using more than one medium. Physical evidence, personal accounts and ecological or technical observations should corroborate one another without each stating the answer.
- Record each clue's stable ID, actual fact, plausible initial interpretation, later corroboration, discovery conditions, region, gameplay payoff and reveal tier. Label hypothesis as hypothesis in the player's notebook. Do not make the narrator falsely certify a convenient lie.
- Keep essential information discoverable through more than one route or evidence source. Optional journals deepen people and motives; collecting every entry is not a progression requirement.
- Reinterpret facts rather than invalidating the player's whole journey. The reveal must preserve the consequence of their choices and repay their attention. A dream/simulation reset or an explanation introduced only in the last scene does not satisfy this brief.
- No lore may require a creature behavior, resource source, vehicle capability or environmental event that the game does not actually support. If scope changes, revise the evidence and canon together.

## Regional guidance

A lead describes a region, landmark, direction, depth band or observable phenomenon. For example, an illustrative lead might mention mineral deposits beneath a split arch where two currents meet; it does not place a marker directly on an undiscovered item. Use recognizable silhouettes, local environmental cues and an annotated journal so the player can reason about where to go.

The notebook retains what was actually learned, the source, unresolved questions and any preparation implied by the evidence. Discovered sites can be named or marked by the player. Offer more than one plausible investigation where geography permits; avoid a single mandatory sequence of exact-coordinate errands.

When the player is stuck, a voluntary hint should first restate known evidence, then make the region or landmark clearer. It should not reveal the twist or silently complete the deduction. Hint behavior and accessibility need playtesting; the recommended guidance does not mean that every player must navigate unaided.

## Tie every important expedition to the world

The intended unit is a question, a preparation decision, an observed environmental rule, a risky commitment and a consequential return. A critical discovery should either change the player's understanding, expand their options or both.

The world/progression team and lore writer jointly maintain an expedition matrix: regional lead → destination/habitat → relevant resource or behavior → equipment choice → evidence → practical reward → new route or question. Use early materials in later meaningful upgrades and make resource habitats teachable. The recipe and evidence must agree about why the research site was built there.

Use geography and capabilities to control what is physically reachable. Lore entries themselves should not become arbitrary keys. If a player reaches a site early, preserve the evidence and adapt its contextual reading; do not erase it or demand that they rediscover it in the intended order. Essential interactions need robust state for save/load and interrupted visits.

The approximately ten-hour main-story target still needs real first-time playtests. Plan a rhythm of orientation, widening investigation, deeper preparation, reinterpretation and payoff. Do not lengthen the story through repeated harvesting, waiting for logs or compulsory travel back across solved routes.

## Authoring and review boundaries

The Astra `xhigh` coordinator sets story direction, approves canon changes, and reconciles the [canon appendix — major story spoilers](spoilers/NARRATIVE_CANON.md) and chronology with geography, resources, vehicle progression and the accepted scope. Assign lore/content authoring and implementation to **Sol 6.1 max**, including canon edits under exclusive path ownership. The Sol lore writer reads the canon and proposes any historical changes to the coordinator before implementing dependent content. Sol graphics workers receive the visual and reveal-tier information needed for their assets and follow the approved history. Lore batches use the same independent non-author Astra/Sol review gate as other implementation.

The main build input, README, PR description and ordinary chat summaries remain readable without revealing the ending. The full build input explicitly references the writer appendix so implementation agents can read the truth. Filenames, scene titles and objective labels should not accidentally disclose it. The appendix is in a public repo and is not private; the separation is a courtesy to readers choosing to avoid spoilers.

Before the final input is ready, review the canon for contradictions, unsupported clues, circular progression, out-of-order discovery and missing-clue recovery. During the build, observe fresh players: what they think happened, why they chose a route, what clues they remember, where they get stuck and whether the reveal feels earned. Agent review establishes internal consistency, not surprise or enjoyment in a human audience.
