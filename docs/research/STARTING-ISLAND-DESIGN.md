## 1. Art direction: four options

**What the island has to do visually:**
- read as a silhouette from the pod at about 200 m;
- make "where can I walk?" obvious at close range;
- explain sea caves, a spring and a deep flooded passage without them looking placed;
- contrast with three neighbours:
  - **the reef:** jade water, shell ribs, coral and amber fans, pale banded limestone;
  - **the hub:** jade tops on petrol flanks, warm lamps, a white mast;
  - **the later Splitspine Isles:** black-green columnar basalt, ochre and "human work". The first island must not use up the Isles' look.

### A · Lifted Reef (recommended)

A raised coral-limestone island, in the style of a *makatea*: an old reef lifted out of the sea.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Palette** | Bleached Coral `#EFE6D0` (fresh rock), Karst Crust `#958F84` (weathered tops), **Notch Shadow** `#33404F` (the waterline undercut), Pandanus `#4F7A3A`, Fern Lime `#9BBE4E`, Lagoon Glass `#7FD6C9`, Sinkhole Blue `#1E4E73`. Accents: Lichen Saffron `#E0A43A` on windward crowns, Hibiscus `#E2604E` (flowers, the station's vane and flags), Whitewash `#F7F4EC` (station). |
| **Landforms** | **Rounded domes undercut at the waterline into "mushrooms".** Their top surfaces are fluted pinnacle karst. **Holes everywhere:** sinkholes (dolines), shafts, windows, a natural arch, a blowhole, sea caves, and a blue shaft that drops far below sea level. **Flat tide benches** at the waterline hold the pools. Fossil coral heads show in the cliff faces. |
| **Vegetation** | Pandanus (stilt roots and starburst crowns, the signature silhouette), low salt scrub on the benches, tree ferns and a strangler fig down in the sinkholes, seagrass in the lagoon, kelp on the north shelf. **Vegetation crowns the domes and fills the holes; the cliffs stay bare.** That keeps sightlines and frame time cheap. |
| **Props** | **Boardwalks and cut steps** (pinnacle karst is too sharp to walk, so paths are diegetic, and broken sections are natural shortcuts or barriers), rope rails, survey cairns, a tide gauge, a hand pump at the spring, rain-catch tanks, and a power cable on posts from the station to the cove. |
| **Storytelling** | **The island is old reef.** You scan a fossil head in the cliff and find the same species alive below. The station studied the spring and the shaft, so its gear is about water and depth. A boardwalk ends at a collapse. Tide marks and a gauge record the water's moods. |
| **Cel read** | **Three value bands** that read at any distance: a bright cap, a mid flank and a **black notch line** at the waterline. Holes read as dark shapes against the light rock, which makes every hole a lure. Flat-shaded domes give big, clean facets. |
| **Contrast** | **Reef:** cool and saturated underwater, while the island is pale cream with deep green above water. **Hub:** the hub is the same reef idea seen from below, petrol and lamplit; the island is daylight and air. **Isles:** pale, round and pitted against dark, columnar and angular. The Isles become the "dark rock, human work" chapter after this "pale rock, discovery" one. |
| **References** | [Rock Islands, Palau](https://culturalheritageonline.com/places/rock-islands-southern-lagoon/) (uplifted limestone undercut into forest-topped mushrooms; UNESCO). [Niue sea tracks and chasms](https://niuepocketguide.com/10-best-sea-tracks-in-niue) (paths through razor makatea, chasm pools, the Limu pools' swim-through arch). [Dean's Blue Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean%27s_Blue_Hole) (a 202 m blue hole that opens into a cavern at about 20 m, a template for the deep passage). [Benagil sea cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benagil) (a limestone sea cave with a collapsed skylight over an inner beach). [Hidden Beach, Marietas](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/hidden-beach-playa-del-almor) (a sky-open crater reached only by swimming a tunnel, the hidden garden's entrance). |

**Risk:** limestone already appears on the reef (the Colonnade) and the hub. **Mitigation:** the island's above-water rock is the cream-and-grey crust with the black notch, which the reef never has. The Colonnade stays pink-banded cream underwater.

### B · Lichen Tor

### Why A

1. **The geology explains every requested place.** Sea caves, an air-pocket chamber, a spring, tide benches, an enclosed lagoon, a hidden sinkhole garden and a flooded shaft to 150 m are *what limestone does*. Nothing has to be justified with a story-only excuse. B and D would need a mine or a cistern to fake the deep passage. C borrows the Rift's lava tubes.
2. **Land and sea interlock.** That interlocking is what makes overlapping threads work spatially. From the ridge you look down a shaft and see water glinting. Later, from inside the sea cave, you look up that same shaft at the sky. Holes are the island's way of whispering "this connects to that", Outer Wilds-style.
3. **A unique silhouette.** The undercut notch is a black line no other place in Siltwake has. It reads at 200 m from the pod, and it makes the island feel like a held breath above the water.
4. **Small art cost.** About eight dome and notch modules, one pandanus, three scrub clumps, a boardwalk kit and a few hero props. It's cheaper than C's foliage or D's unique architecture.
5. **Borrow the best of the others:**
   - from B, the saffron lichen on windward crowns (complementary pop) and named rocks in the journal;
   - from D, terraces *only inside the hidden garden* (a Heligan-style walled plot in a sinkhole);
   - from C, a single mist moment: morning haze in the garden sinkhole.

## 2. Exploration patterns for a small, dense island

| Pattern | Evidence from games | On this island |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Landmarks at three scales** | BotW's field team uses **"triangles" at three sizes**: large ones are landmarks, medium ones block the view to make you wonder what's behind, and small ones set the tempo. Odd-shaped triangles hide Korok secrets. Rectangles hide things completely ([CEDEC 2017 translation](https://gist.github.com/idbrii/e39fe96279aa1670319bfa521d907399); [summary](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2017/10/zelda_breath_of_the_wilds_ingenious_design_is_all_about_triangles_apparently)). | **Large:** the summit dome and pillar, the station mast. **Medium:** each dome hides the next cove. **Small:** pinnacles, boulders and pandanus at 10–20 m. **Odd shapes get the secrets:** a dome with a hole, a pandanus growing *out of* a shaft. |
| **Gravity, not towers** | BotW first used towers to string events together; playtesters found that too linear. "Gravity", meaning structures of varied visibility and importance in different directions, spread players out (same source). | Three pulls are visible from the beach in different directions: the **station** (west, up), the **crane** (south, round the Elbow) and the **lagoon** (north, flat). There's no single trail that obviously "is the game". |
| **One always-visible goal** | A Short Hike keeps Hawk Peak visible as the goal, then uses paths, caves and natural barriers to balance freedom and guidance ([GDC 2020](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A); [video, Hawk Peak section about 14:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sdOkIagytlE)). | The lookout pillar is visible from everywhere outside the sinkholes, so the summit is the island's Hawk Peak. Climbing it is *orientation*, not progress. |
| **A visible lure you can't have yet** | Subnautica's ship drew players by silhouette before it was explorable ([Unknown Worlds](https://steamcommunity.com/games/264710/announcements/detail/517126407355534931)). | The **blowhole spout** (a moving lure seen from the pod), the **sealed door in the hillside**, and the **green hole** of the garden seen from the lookout with no obvious way down. |
| **Loops and shortcuts** | BotW: you can always go over or around a triangle. A Short Hike: paths fold back on themselves. | Every major place has **at least two ways in.** **One-way shortcuts open from the far side:** kick down the station ladder to the tide bench; drop the garden's rope ladder from inside. That's the Dark Souls-style "door opens behind you" without combat. |
| **Hidden nooks** | Tunic's secrets range from "behind the waterfall" to the obscure, and Shouldice values secrets that make you try something new ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/09/21/the-creation-of-tunics-invaluable-in-game-manual/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/tunic-s-developer-on-creating-the-in-game-manual-full-of-mysteries)). | Behind the blowhole, under the boardwalk, inside the arch's thickness, at the bottom of the spring. Each holds a **small, specific** reward: a cache, a scan, a view or a note. |
| **Verticality** | The Great Plateau is a raised table you explore before leaving ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plateau)). | 55 m above sea level and 140 m below. Water and air alternate: shelf → beach → ridge → shaft → cave → air pocket → garden. |
| **Something every 20–30 m** | BotW's small triangles serve the tempo. Siltwake's pacing rule is something new every 15–20 minutes at the macro scale. | **The micro rule:** from any standing point, there's **one unvisited thing within 30 m** and **one landmark beyond 100 m** in view. The beach-to-lookout trail below follows it. |
| **Teaching without tutorials** | The Witness introduces each rule with a minimal example, then a harder one, with no words ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witness_(2016_video_game)); [TIME](https://time.com/4191490/the-witness-review/)). Tunic's manual points at secrets. | **A minimal first example before the real thing:** a 3 m crevice with a glint before the Throat; a knee-deep tide pool with one fish before the lagoon; a half-buried tin with one seed before the garden. **The survival manual is diegetic and annotated by hand, like Tunic's.** |
| **No obvious critical path** | Outer Wilds splits its mystery into four "curiosity webs" you can uncover in any order ([Beachum podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/outer-wilds-alex-beachums-cool-weird-emotional-prototypes/id1671350556?i=1000702968300); [GDC 2020 talk announcement](https://gdconf.com/article/attend-gdc-and-learn-how-outer-wilds-nailed-curiosity-driven-game-design/)). | **Three threads, three directions, any two needed** (section 3). The workshop isn't at the landing beach. The trails are a ring with spokes, not a line. |

| Distance | What's there |
| --- | --- |
| 0 m | Beached dinghy and trailhead cairn |
| 25 m | Fluted Steps boardwalk, broken in one span: climb round or jump |

## 3. Overlapping threads and the unfinished ones

**How real games handle "do these things to proceed":**

| Game | Rule | What it teaches us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Super Mario 64 | **N of M:** big star doors need 8, 30, 50, then 70 of 120 stars ([Mario Wiki](https://mario.fandom.com/wiki/Big_Star_Door)) | Thresholds give freedom, but a counter is abstract. |
| Dark Souls | **Both, any order:** two Bells of Awakening, one "up above", one "far below" ([wiki](https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Bells_of_Awakening)) | Order freedom without redundancy, so a stuck player stays stuck. |
| BotW, Great Plateau | **All four shrines, any order**, then the paraglider | Order freedom works when every thread is short. |
| Subnautica | **Redundant sources:** the same blueprint's fragments sit in several wrecks ([community wiki](https://subnautica.fandom.com/wiki/Fragments)) | Different places lead to the same output: "any two of three" in spatial form. |
| Outer Wilds | Knowledge gates. The ship log flags "There's more to explore here", **imperfectly**: some optional entries never flag ([guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3500382207)) | Unfinished threads stay visible as questions, which is what Siltwake's journal leads already do. |
| Return of the Obra Dinn | Fates confirm **in sets of three** to stop guessing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)) | Batching hides which single item was wrong. Don't batch an exit gate. |

**Verdict on the owner's lean: yes to "any two of three" plus "unfinished threads come back later", under five conditions.**

1. **Make the gate diegetic and visible before it's met.** Don't use a counter. The workshop's feed board has **two empty sockets** and three cable runs, each labelled with where it comes from. The player sees "two of three" on day one.
2. **Each thread pays its own unique reward,** so the third isn't wasted.
3. **The threads overlap like a Venn diagram:** progress in one makes another cheaper. Most players finish about two and a half.
4. **No hard locks:** every required tool has a non-cave source, as the content bible already requires.
5. **The third feed is a bonus, not a chore.** It powers the cove's charging cradle (faster recharge and repair) instead of gating anything.

**The three threads:**

| Thread | Starts from | Chain (record → recipe → resource → tool → need → place) | Teaches | Unique reward | Feed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lamp** | The **survival manual** in the reef wreck | Manual describes the lamp → glow sacs from the tide pools plus glass from the Fluted Steps scar → lamp → its margin note: "cells cached where it's darkest, the Throat" → the Throat | Scanning, crafting, darkness, the lamp, finding exits | The lamp (needed for the Isles undercroft later) | A cell bank from the cache |
| **The Scientist** | The **field station** and its journal | The journal asks "where does the spring go?" → follow the stream → the Bell's air pocket → crawl up → hidden garden → seeds, the plot notes, and the mast's spare vane stored dry → repair the station mast | Leads as questions, breathing in an air pocket, reading landmarks (the lookout plate), cultivation | The garden and seeds | The station mast, via the cable on posts |
| **The Tide** | The **workshop cove**, found by its crane | Workshop log: "tide wheel fouled again" → the lagoon mouth wheel is choked with wrack → craft a cutter from wreck scrap → clear it in the mouth's current, then net the lagoon fish | Tools, currents as obstacles, catching and cooking fish | The fish net and the cove fish trap | The tide wheel |

**Where the threads overlap:**
- The lamp makes the Bell's crawl comfortable.
- Garden fibre makes the net.
- The lookout's sighting plate has notches for the Throat, the wheel and the station.
- The station's cable leads to the cove.
- The manual's lamp page is useful in the Bell.

**Unfinished threads come back:**
- Their leads stay open in the journal, phrased as the characters' questions.
- Later needs re-raise them. The undercroft's darkness resurfaces "the manual said…"; a missing cutter shows up at the Isles' wrack. Each time the island is about 200 m from home.

**Garden (open question):** the owner's proposal is right. The hidden plot is the *found* garden. You restore **one bed there** (plant it before you leave, harvest when you're back), and its notes unlock a planter on the cove's sunny ledge, which becomes *your* garden. If the plot is left alone it still grows a little, so there's a small reason to return.

## 5. Blockout plan

The sketch is [`starting-island/blockout-map.svg`](starting-island/blockout-map.svg). Local metres: the origin is the island centre, +x is east and +z is north.

**Placement:** the island centre is at about world **(-205, 0, -5)**, **205 m west-south-west of the pod** at (0, 0, 14).
- **North is out:** the world envelope ends at z = 160, so there's no room north of the pod.
- **South** (about (40, -200)) would take the hub spur's root and the Basin edge, so it's the alternative.
- **West-south-west** keeps a shallow learning shelf all round, puts the south flank on the **Hearth drop** so the deep passage can surface in the gully, and leaves the Colonnade and pumphouse as the next ring out.

**Reef proposals this absorbs** (they're typed but not wired):
- the nursery flats become the **lagoon floor**;
- the grazer scar moves to the island's **east shelf** beside the wreck;
- the Fan terraces' first-cave beat becomes **the Throat**.

**Size:**
- dry land about **200 × 180 m**, summit **55 m**;
- a shelf 2–15 m deep extends 30–60 m beyond the shore;
- the south flank drops to the shelf break at 30–40 m;
- below that, the gully.

**Loops:**
- **The ring:** beach → lagoon shore → spring → station → lookout → ridge → beach (about 450 m).
- **The water ring:** beach → wreck → round the north shelf → the Bell → the tide bench (all swimmable).
- **Shortcuts that open from the far side:** the station ladder and the garden ladder.
- **The cable line:** station → lookout shoulder → cove. You can follow it in either direction.

**Sightlines to protect:**
- **From the pod:** the summit pillar, the station mast, the blowhole spout, the wreck's pennant and the crane tip. The cove itself stays hidden.
- **From the lookout:**
  - the pod at 78°, with the Splitspine stack directly behind it at 81° and 670 m: home and the next region on one line;
  - the **hub beacon at 134°, about 760 m**;
  - the Colonnade at 294°, 250 m;
  - the gully at 185°;
  - the garden's green hole about 60 m below.
- **From the beach:** the trail cairns up the spine, and the station roof.
