# World layout and progression: a basin of loops

2026-10-20 · Game-design research · Spoiler-free Siltwake recommendations

**Recommendation: build option A first.** Keep the 2.0 × 1.8 km footprint, extend a shallow refuge toward its centre, and turn the regional sequence into three connected expedition loops. Reserve option B's larger, interleaved basin for a later scope decision. Cohesion comes from recognizable neighbours, several approaches and improving journeys home; increasing surface area alone supplies none of these.

This is research and a layout proposal, not a specification amendment or an accepted playable milestone. Read against [WORLD_PROGRESSION](../WORLD_PROGRESSION.md), [EXPEDITION_INTEGRATION](../EXPEDITION_INTEGRATION.md), [FLEET_AND_SURVIVAL](../FLEET_AND_SURVIVAL.md) and current [build direction](../build/DIRECTION.md). No `docs/spoilers/` files were read. The brief places Canopy on the north shelf; WORLD_PROGRESSION calls it eastern. The proposals use a northern Canopy with an eastern descending arm, leaving that coordinate reconciliation to integration. No owner map image was attached to this checkout; Subnautica adjacency was checked against the linked wiki map and biome descriptions.

## 1. What Subnautica's map actually does

**Think irregular rings with fingers, holes and a second world underneath.** Safe Shallows sits near the centre, with Lifepod 5 providing the initial reference point. Kelp Forest and four separate Grassy Plateaus patches form an interrupted early belt; more demanding habitats lie beyond and between them. This is a useful reading of the map, not a claim that the developers generated perfect difficulty rings. The playable crater is roughly 3.3–4.3 km across; the in-world “2 km” description should not be used as its measured diameter. [S1–S3]

Three adjacencies show why a north-to-south chapter strip would miss the design:

- **Grassy Plateaus → Crash Zone:** the 40–200 m plateaus border the ship's dangerous neighbourhood, as well as Safe Shallows, Kelp Forest, Dunes and other habitats. A familiar resource trip can expose a frightening neighbouring route. Horizontal proximity does not mean equal risk. [S3]
- **Northeastern Mushroom Forest → Bulb Zone:** the forest's 75–250 m shelves fall toward the Bulb Zone; their caves connect. Predators can wander across these borders. The transition has physical continuity, rather than a clean level-select boundary. [S4]
- **Early seabed → deeper interior:** Jellyshroom Cave lies beneath Safe Shallows, Kelp Forest and Grassy Plateaus, with four entrances around 90–110 m leading toward a floor reaching roughly 305 m. Later, the Lost River has four entrances distributed through different surface/deep biomes and spans approximately 525–1,065 m. Increasing capability makes familiar geography newly usable. [S5–S6]

**Depth and distance do different jobs.** Distance establishes a new neighbourhood, exposure and hauling cost. Depth increases commitment: the surface is farther away, the light changes, and a vehicle's pressure rating becomes decisive. Cave roofs remove the option of a straight ascent. Consequently, a nearby hole can be a later expedition than a distant shallow island. There is no single scalar “difficulty = distance from spawn.” [S3–S8]

**Orientation operates at several scales.** The Aurora's enormous surface silhouette provides a persistent directional anchor and a desirable destination. Below water, plateau pillars, mushroom crowns, kelp walls, cave mouths and distinctive deep landmarks provide local frames of reference. Radio leads send the player to places worth investigating, often beside something else worth noticing: Lifepod 17 is near Jellyshroom entrances; Lifepod 2 draws attention toward a deep approach. These are invitations to explore a neighbourhood, rather than a complete route through the game. Cleveland explicitly describes radio signals as structure for a sandbox; his GDC slides separate directed crafting goals from player-created goals and depict several story threads. [S1, S3, S6, S9–S10]

**Home remains central because it remains useful.** A base near the early-biome junction has familiar supplies and branches in several directions. Cleveland's loot-design essay explicitly links recognizable regional materials to keeping old areas relevant as the player descends. A fixed main base is nevertheless the player's choice, not an enforced centre: forward bases and the Cyclops can shift the practical hub toward the current expedition. [S11–S12]

**Short returns are earned, and not universal.** At the wiki's approximately 11.25 m/s single-axis Seamoth speed, a clear 500 m leg takes 44 seconds and 1 km takes 89 seconds, before turns, searching, hazards or docking. A central start avoids repeatedly traversing the whole crater. Multiple entrances, player beacons and a mobile base reduce rediscovery and resupply costs. Very late activated transport links also compress revisits; they are not the early world's organizing principle. Deep cave trips can still be long. Siltwake should copy the reduction in repeated work, not promise that every deep-to-pod return takes a minute. [S7, S12–S14]

## 2. Gates that allow dangerous neighbours

| Gate | Subnautica example | Spatial consequence to transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pressure and mobile air | Seamoth: 200 → 300 → 500 → 900 m. Cyclops: 500 → 900 → 1,300 → 1,700 m. Prawn: 900 → 1,300 → 1,700 m. Powered vehicles replenish the diver's oxygen. | A player can see or visit a border before sustaining an expedition beyond it. An upgrade changes the usable cross-section of existing terrain. [S7–S8, S12] |
| Tools and access | Scanning fragments unlocks equipment; the laser cutter opens sealed wreck doors; radiation equipment enables the contaminated ship approach. | Put later interiors inside earlier neighbourhoods. Make the missing capability and retreat understandable. [S7, S15–S16] |
| Environmental risk | Predators, dark water, cave roofs and brine add hazards independent of crush depth. | Provide a scouting ledge and a safer approach beside the demanding route; depth alone should not solve every hazard. [S4–S6] |
| Information and motivation | Radio signals have time, exploration or crafting triggers; some transmissions explicitly warn about unsafe depth. | Reveal a reason to prepare and return. Signals guide attention; most exterior terrain is not locked until its signal plays. [S9–S10] |

These are mostly **soft gates**, with specific hard interior locks. A skilled player can make an early dive, park a vehicle at its limit, or discover something out of the anticipated order. That is part of the openness. Preserve the discovery; control sustained access through range, pressure, clearance and preparation. Do not attempt pacing by making every hazardous biome unreachable until a chapter flag changes. This is the design inference from the mechanisms above, not a claim of perfectly enforced Subnautica sequencing.

**The world edge is a different boundary.** Subnautica drops the crater into resource-poor open water, gives a PDA warning, then escalates adult Ghost Leviathan presence. It communicates that the productive ecosystem has ended; it is not simply another resource tier. A distant technical teleport exists, but should not be mistaken for the intended encounter. [S17] For Siltwake, propose an outer escarpment, disappearing benthic life, adverse current cues and a clear navigation warning before a bounded forced retreat. Keep the distant ocean visible. Do not hide mandatory stock or disabled-vessel recovery beyond this boundary, or create a new lethal predator solely to police a rectangle.

## 3. Comparisons worth transferring

**Below Zero: density improves local richness, but can weaken both scale and readability.** IGN's reviewer praises memorable silhouettes, elaborate hidden areas and less empty space, while reporting disappointment at reaching the smaller map's edge and finding less deep-world scale. PC Gamer praises Twisty Bridges and the alien habitats, but finds complex topography disorienting, similar-looking outcrops hard to distinguish and searched nests difficult to recognize. Its Seatruck criticism also concerns slow movement with modules. These are attributed assessments, not proof that small maps fail. [S18–S19]

Rock Paper Shotgun gives the complementary reading: expeditions radiate from safety like spokes; player-placed beacons make meaningful cartography; explicit story directions can help players who previously lost their way. Its reviewer still chose to ignore those directions and investigate independently. [S20]

Player responses contain the same tension. One positive Steam review, reporting extensive original-game experience, praises the environments but says slower travel makes the player feel slower rather than the map larger. Another detailed negative review objects to abrupt transitions and a compressed deep journey. These are individual qualitative reports, not representative polling; their causal explanations and numerical map claims are not adopted as facts. [S21–S22] **Transfer:** keep density, distinctive habitats and voluntary guidance; budget turning room, visual separation and vehicle speed together. Do not compensate for a smaller world by slowing ordinary travel or making every route a tangled tunnel.

| Other game | Evidence and transferable spatial principle | Siltwake application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Outer Wilds** | Beachum's GDC presentation makes curiosity → exploration → knowledge the loop. Slides 31–38 emphasize conspicuous physical phenomena and information the player chooses to pull; slides 52–60 connect clues to places and distinguish solving an inference from remembering every fact. Mobius describes handcrafted, changing locations and directional audio tools. [S23–S24] | A visible anomaly raises a question; a local record points toward another region; the notebook retains the connection. Revisit a familiar route with a new understanding. ## 4. Preserve Siltwake's capability geography

The integration contract already supplies the right dependencies. Both options preserve them:

| Preceding capability | Required reachable shelf | What it enables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic preparation; suit to 150 m | Reef/islands, then upper Canopy ≤150 m | Early materials support the first 350 m reinforcement. |
| 350 m configuration | Rift ceramic 250–285 m; core 280–320 m on cool shoulders | 450 m service/pressure capability. |
| 450 m configuration | Divide lattice 360–390 m on flushed ledges | 800 m craft **and personal exit protection**. |
| 800 m service configuration | Meridian 520–780 m | Deep investigation and service tasks using earlier material families. |

Keep Dart 180–350 m, Creel 240–800 m through refits, and Hearthline 400–550 m. Retain moorings at Canopy 160 m, Basin 240 m, Rift 300 m, Divide 390 m and upgraded Meridian staging 520 m. Mantle remains an alternative to the deep-refitted Creel, not a compulsory purchase. The central hub must support both independent Creel/service-stop expeditions and Hearthline-supported sorties. A beautiful new shortcut cannot become the only survivable first approach. [Local integration contract]

### Travel arithmetic for both options

Current checkout constants: swimming 3.4 m/s, Dart cruise 8 m/s, Creel cruise 5 m/s and quiet 2.5 m/s, Hearthline 3 m/s. Vehicle vertical thrust is scaled to 0.75 of its ordinary speed. These are code inputs, not observed journey averages: [step.ts](../../src/sim/step.ts), [fleet.ts](../../src/sim/fleet.ts).

Use a conservative segmented estimate, **horizontal route length / cruise speed + vertical change / vertical speed**, then allow 20–40% for ordinary approaches and turns; price stealth separately. This avoids quoting a straight-line diagonal as a navigable route. At 5 m/s, 250–600 m horizontal legs take 50–120 seconds; at 8 m/s they take 31–75 seconds. Thus the world plan's 45–120-second observation/decision cadence must be tuned per craft, not assumed from its distances. A 150 m quiet Creel segment alone takes 60 seconds. Swimming sites should generally keep refuge/vehicle hops around 30–80 m, approximately 9–24 seconds horizontally, with task time and ascent budgeted separately.

## 5. Option A — current footprint, improved

**Shape:** retain northwest Hearth Reef and the three Splitspine islands, northern Canopy, central Pale Basin, western Ember Rift, southern Mirror Divide and southeast Meridian. Extend a narrow **Hearth Reef spur** southeast to a 45–60 m crown near the map's centre, roughly 1.0 km east and 0.85 km south of the northwest corner. This crown is part of the existing reef, not an eighth required region. Its sheltered upper edge is the fixed logistics hub; below it lies the Basin's 150–380 m bowl. The starting pod stays northwest, connected by a straightforward shallow route.

**Word schematic, north up:** the pod and island workshop occupy the upper-left; Canopy spans the upper arc and curls down the right. The shallow hub sits just north of the Basin's centre. Ember Rift cuts into the bowl from the left; Mirror Divide reaches up from below in two ridge fingers. Meridian occupies the lower-right and extends beneath the Canopy's eastern arm. Connect hub → Canopy → Basin → hub; hub → Rift → Divide → Basin → hub; and Divide → Meridian → eastern Canopy escarpment → Basin. These are route loops at different depths, not permission to pass through the deepest area in starter gear.

Four layout changes do most of the work:

1. **Make the centre desirable early.** Show the crown from Canopy's edge and the workshop approach; provide a charging/rest stop and open staging water beside the established fabrication network. Link pod to crown with two recognizable intermediate reef ribs. Keep food, repair stock and personal refuge at the northwest pod; transfer ordinary cargo conveniently when the central stop is restored. Base placement here is a recommendation for authored services and the earned mobile refuge, not a new promise of unrestricted modular base building.
2. **Intermix through shelves and overlaps.** Bring a cool Rift finger toward the Basin's western rim; its first stock remains at 250–320 m. Give the southern Divide a visible upper ridge beside a 150–220 m Basin terrace, with lattice still down at 360–390 m. Under the eastern Canopy, expose a deep escarpment opening toward Meridian while keeping the upper lab and early material route ≤150 m. Each early region now reveals a future trip without supplying its final protection for free.
3. **Give each major crossing two routes.** Pair broad carrier lanes with narrower small-craft flanks. Restore a Basin–Rift shelf connection and a Rift–Divide cooled flank after useful investigations. Make their return-facing exits easy to recognize. The Meridian–Canopy outer escarpment stays deep, as already specified; it must not become a pressure bypass. Add a direct climb toward the central crown only after reaching shallower Basin water. Carrier channels need visible clearance, turning bowls and unobstructed launch space.
4. **Build a chain of lures.** Splitspine's damaged mast and split sea stack give surface bearings; the central twin reef ribs frame departures; fallen Basin dishes point toward the split chimney; Divide gateworks silhouette against clear upper water; Canopy's distinct eastern root arch marks the deep return. Reveal a next landmark around a ridge while retaining a recognizable way back. Give important junctions a distant silhouette, a local shape/flow cue and a player-marker opportunity. Signals name a bearing, depth band and observable condition; exact destination markers follow discovery or an optional sharpened hint.

Proposed travel budgets below include the stated turn/approach allowance but exclude searching, harvesting, repairs and dialogue. Lengths are authored route targets, not surveyed current terrain.

| Journey | Horizontal route; depth change | Expected ordinary travel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northwest pod/workshop → central crown | 700–900 m; about 0–40 m | Dart about 2–3 min; Creel about 3–4.5 min. A convenient return, not a mandatory trip after every sortie. |
| Crown → Canopy investigation | 300–450 m; 50 → 150 m | Dart about 1–1.7 min; Creel about 1.7–2.7 min, before any quiet segment. |
| Crown → cooled Rift shoulder | 500–650 m; 50 → 300 m | Creel about 3.3–4.6 min; one intermediate Basin decision/observation. |
| Basin anchorage → Divide ledge | 400–600 m; 240 → 390 m | Creel about 2.4–3.7 min; include a second route decision rather than one empty leg. |
| Meridian 520 m staging → 780 m worksite | 250–450 m; 260 m descent | Deep Creel about 2.4–3.8 min each way, plus work/stealth. Hearthline stays at the shelf. |
| Deep worksite → central crown | 800–1,100 m; 780 → 50 m | Deep Creel about 7–10 min. Avoid routine demands for this whole journey; use the 520/390/240 m service chain. |

**Why home stays relevant:** the crown is the shortest shared junction for ordinary early/mid logistics, while the pod remains the recovery anchor and islands retain workshop/food visits. Beyond the Divide, home advances through service stops or Hearthline. At 3 m/s, moving the carrier 1 km already takes 5.6 minutes before ascent and turns; it should relocate for a group of sorties, not shuttle home after every pickup. Fixed stops must provide enough reserve and cargo support for the carrier-independent strategy too.

## 6. Option B — a larger interleaved basin

**Envelope:** approximately **3.2 × 2.8 km**, still reaching 780 m. Its rectangular area is 8.96 km² versus today's 3.6 km²: about **2.5× the authoring envelope**, not a demonstrated need for 2.5× the content. This is a scope expansion to consider after a complete campaign, not a new construction requirement.

**Word schematic, north up:** place Hearth Reef and the initial pod in the inner centre-northwest, with the three Splitspine islands forming an asymmetric northern arc around home. Establish the main fixed workshop/anchorage near the reef's centre at 45–60 m. Wrap Canopy in separate northern and eastern patches; let Pale Basin curve around the centre's south and west. Ember Rift cuts northeast from the southwest rim toward that Basin; Mirror Divide bends from the south toward an eastern ridge spur. Meridian lies below the southeast arc, with deep approaches from both Divide and the eastern Canopy escarpment. A remembered shallow centre therefore remains close in plan view even when a deep journey is difficult in section.

Use **broken bands**, with the following distances measured from the central anchorage:

- **Inner 0–450 m:** Hearth Reef and workshop-facing Splitspine shores, 0–90 m; a Canopy tongue begins around 55 m. The remaining island landings remain useful destinations, not replacements for the safe pod.
- **Middle 350–850 m:** Canopy 55–220 m and Pale Basin 150–380 m interleave. A Rift lip reaches into the west at 250–320 m; an upper Divide ridge appears on the east. Several early-to-mid routes can be attempted from home without circumnavigating the map.
- **Outer 700–1,300 m:** Rift 250–540 m and Divide 360–650 m form separate demanding lobes, with safe approach pockets between them. Keep some shallow shelves on the outer rim so that “far away” is not invariably “late game.”
- **Deep layer under middle/outer bands:** Meridian 520–780 m, with approaches through different neighbours. Overlap is vertical as well as horizontal; it is not a final biome beyond the last southern border. Preserve the distinction between exterior access and facility-service requirements.

Three **new optional habitat candidates** fill specific spatial gaps: *Glass Shoals* (northwest outer shelf, 40–140 m), an exposed sightline and island-supply route; *Chime Escarpment* (northeast, 180–420 m), a fractured wall with small-craft passages and a deep-route overlook; *Ash Apron* (southwest, 280–500 m), a cool depositional flank linking Rift to Divide. Names and ecology are proposals. Initially these could be sub-biomes of existing regions; promoting them to new top-level regions changes the seven-region scope and needs a coordinator/owner decision. Give them existing regional material variants and optional observations, not new mandatory material, hull or story tiers. Keep exactly three principal islands.

**Base placement and returns:** offer the central 45–60 m fixed anchorage, Basin staging at 240 m and forward stops at 300/390/520 m. Keep early powered destinations 300–600 m apart; deep access mouths roughly 700–1,100 m of navigable horizontal route from home, with intermediate landmarks/stops. At Creel cruise, a 600 m same-depth leg takes 2 minutes; 1,100 m plus 340 m descent to the Divide takes about 6.2–7.2 minutes including allowance. A 2 km same-depth crossing takes 6.7 minutes before allowance, or 11.1 minutes in Hearthline. Use 3–5-minute carrier staging moves between nearby moorings where geometry permits; do not require repeated end-to-end relocations. Deep local sorties should retain option A's 2.4–3.8-minute one-way budget from the 520 m shelf.

The additional area buys alternative bearings, quieter outer habitats and a stronger sense of separation. It must earn that cost through distinct expeditions. Enlarging every gap while keeping the same destination graph would make the current progression less cohesive.

## 7. Decision and next evidence

Choose **A now; preserve B as an evolution path**. A addresses the weak spatial sequence without reopening world scale or multiplying mandatory habitats while most campaign production remains. Build the linked crown–Canopy–Basin loop, one Rift/Divide interleave and the deep staging/return connection before filling intervening water. This follows the build direction's priority of a rough complete campaign and a representative scale proof.

Evaluate with fresh players: can they name home, the next landmark and their retreat route; notice a future deep entrance from an early shelf; choose a second approach; and return on the preceding tier with the integration contract's **at least 25% oxygen/power reserve**? Record actual first-trip and solved-route times separately, including quiet approaches, cargo and docking. Target a noticeable reduction in repeat-trip work through learned routes and service access; a provisional 25–40% time reduction is a playtest hypothesis, not an acceptance fact. Check both fleet strategies and starter-built recovery access at every hazardous depth. If the world feels small, first establish whether landmarks reveal everything too soon or routes lack distinct decisions before adding kilometres. Boetel's postmortem emphasizes iterative feedback; neither these comparisons nor travel arithmetic establishes ten hours of enjoyable play. [S27]
