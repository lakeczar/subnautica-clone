# Caves and underground spaces for Siltwake

Research: **2026-10-20**; code baseline **`79a227cc5224aa019cd82451ded48fc4d7ca882e`**. Research/proposal only. No production changes, game/browser run or performance measurement. No `docs/spoilers/` files read.

**Recommendation: retain the heightfield outdoors and add a few authored cave volumes that replace terrain locally, with baked mesh recipes and a permanent, renderer-independent collision shell. Start with box-built chambers, roofs and broad passages using the existing continuous solid sweeps.** Add convex wedges only if the first playable cave demonstrates that boxes cannot meet handling/art needs. Do not introduce a world-wide voxel system, runtime CSG or a second Babylon-owned movement authority.

## What caves should contribute

Unknown Worlds explicitly describes below-seabed spaces ranging from claustrophobic passages to bioluminescent caverns and lava chambers, with oxygen and darkness as expedition risks. Its equipment loop connects deeper exploration to rarer resources. Those are developer descriptions, not evidence that every dark tunnel is enjoyable. [D1]

| Reference | Observed/documented contribution | Application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Jellyshroom Cave** | A distinct luminous ecosystem below familiar shallow biomes; multiple entrances, magnetite/shale and a habitat investigation. Crabsnakes make apparent cover dangerous. Its transcribed habitat log explicitly values an emergency exit, distance from predators, stable foundations and nearby materials. [D3] | A cave can be a memorable destination, not just a connector. Put a recognizable chamber beyond the entrance; distinguish a reliable refuge from an animal den. |
| **Lost River** | Several outer biomes feed a connected subterranean region. Brine channels, fossils and the Tree Cove differentiate branches; Tree Cove supplies a quieter contrast before deeper descent. Developer updates explicitly connect the Bone Fields, junction, forest and lava approach, and later expand the tunnels. [D2, D4] | Landmarks and loops make underground geography learnable. Use a quiet staging chamber between hazards; allow a discovered alternate entrance to shorten returns. |
| **Inactive Lava Zone** | A large underground biome, reached through Lost River, combines heat, pressure, volcanic landmarks, advanced resources and investigation. It is not simply a narrow tunnel. [D2, D5] | One spacious deep chamber can carry the sense of descent. Keep Siltwake's first ceramic/core outside protection-dependent routes. |
| **Kelp Forest caves** | Resource pockets and connections under an already useful habitat; stingers, crashfish and darkness complicate entry. The transcribed PDA warns of loss of orientation followed by asphyxiation. [D6] | Teach overhead obstruction early: rising is no longer an escape. Make the first loop short, its exit recognizable from both directions, and its rewards optional. |
| **Grassy Plateaus caves** | Smaller flora-rich pockets reward looking under the surface; bioluminescent vegetation coexists with local hazards and uncommon plants. [D7] | Small caves need a distinct ecological or resource purpose; they need not all become story dungeons. |
| **Sparse Reef sanctuary** | The Deep Sparse Reef cave hides an optional constructed destination, contrasting quiet natural approach with architecture. This refers to the sanctuary cache, not Below Zero's sanctuary. [D8] | A concealed, story-neutral alcove or survey shelter can reward observation without becoming a mandatory clue gate. |
| **Below Zero** | Arctic Kelp cave nests connect animal behavior to scannable equipment fragments; Deep Twisty Bridges mixes oxygen plants, fragment hunting and predators; Crystal Caves stages striking crystalline rooms and a deeper connection. Developer descriptions independently establish the crystalline environment, predators and modular Seatruck. [D9–D12] | Connect cave rewards to ecology. Stage a craft at a mouth and continue by diver where appropriate; extra modules/large hulls should have visible reasons to stay outside. Do not assume natural oxygen plants are part of Siltwake. |

**Navigation and handling pitfalls are design risks, not a player-frequency survey.** Similar branches, vertical entrances and featureless darkness can turn oxygen management into blind searching. Tight vehicle turns can convert intended tension into snagging or an unrecoverable wreck. Use asymmetric landmarks, visible entrance silhouettes, a readable floor/ceiling distinction, short first excursions, turning bays and explicit craft parking. Keep mandatory first access viable on the exterior route; the shortcut rewards learning. Test the return view and a lamp-off retreat, not just the approach screenshot.

Below Zero's Pathfinder Tool explicitly marks routes back out of caves; its in-game description is transcribed by the wiki. [D13] Prefer Siltwake's existing discovered-site markers, notebook and authored physical route cues first. A breadcrumb tool would be additional product scope, not an assumed prerequisite.

Other useful comparisons:

- **Deep Rock Galactic:** the developer gives the Scout a lighting role and emphasizes narrow tunnels, large chambers and traversal tools. Borrow readable room/passage rhythm and deliberate illumination; its destructive terrain and combat are different requirements. [D14]
- **Scanner Sombre:** revealing cave geometry with LIDAR makes information itself a reward. Borrow readable silhouettes and optional surveying; do not make every movement depend on scanning. [D15]
- **Diluvion: Resubmerged:** its developer specifically calls out revised landmarks, navigation and pathfinding alongside submarine/depth upgrades. This supports treating navigation as a first-class underwater system; it does not prove a particular cave collision technique. [D16]

## What the checkout actually supports

The linked source is the implementation evidence; older stream notes sometimes describe earlier states.

- [`worldFloor.ts`](../../src/content/regions/worldFloor.ts) composes the authored exterior with a bit-preserved legacy floor. [`terrain.ts`](../../src/content/regions/terrain.ts) supplies trenches, ridges and pads. A steep cliff is still single-valued in X/Z.
- **Wiring prerequisite:** [`runtime/app.ts`](../../src/runtime/app.ts) currently constructs `createWorldQueries()` without arguments. That defaults to `SOLIDS` and `surfaceHeight` in [`sim/world.ts`](../../src/sim/world.ts), while [`biomes/pack.ts`](../../src/render/biomes/pack.ts) supplies `worldFloor` to art. This checkout does **not** demonstrate full-world `worldFloor` collision composition merely because both functions exist. Cave work must compose one physical world into runtime, recovery and validation; do not silently assume that prerequisite is finished.
- `moveCapsule` continuously sweeps solids, slides and conservatively stops unresolved travel. Despite the public name, [`actorBox`](../../src/sim/ribCollision.ts) uses a **fixed-axis box**, including optional hull half-width/length; reef ribs have specialized segment-capsule geometry. Preserve these actor envelopes initially. A true rounded diver capsule is a separate change.
- `clearance`, `supportHeight`, `lineOfSight`, `hullFloor`, `moveHull` and `sweptHullClearance` all depend on the ground/solid contracts. LOS tests solids and samples the floor every 0.15 m. Hull movement slices terrain travel at 0.2 m. [`step.ts`](../../src/sim/step.ts) also reads ground/support directly. Cutting render triangles alone leaves invisible terrain blocking a cave.
- [`spatialIndex.ts`](../../src/world/spatialIndex.ts) has a 32 m X/Z broad phase with exact Y candidate filtering and stable roster ordering. World collision indexes rosters of at least 48 solids. [`access.ts`](../../src/content/regions/access.ts), [`drape.ts`](../../src/content/regions/drape.ts) and [`rescue.ts`](../../src/content/regions/rescue.ts) still validate against a floor function and box corridors. [`recovery.ts`](../../src/sim/recovery.ts) uses actual hull sweeps. Both validators and runtime must learn about ceilings.
- S1's [`ChunkDescriptor`](../../src/world/types.ts) has layer IDs, but [`map.ts`](../../src/world/map.ts) **rejects overlapping vertical layer bounds** and `regionAt` uses X/Z footprints. Existing layer-0 chunks encompass the exterior; adding a cave as overlapping layer 1 is not plug-and-play. Start by including caves in the same column recipe and expanding its Y bounds. Use separate cave-room IDs for navigation/optics.
- [`kit/types.ts`](../../src/render/kit/types.ts), [`recipe.ts`](../../src/render/kit/recipe.ts) and [`loader.ts`](../../src/render/kit/loader.ts) already accept arbitrary typed-array meshes, split uploads, thin instances and deterministic rebuilds. Physics need not depend on Babylon meshes. S1 residency defaults to 256 MiB CPU and 256 MiB GPU; those are accounting caps, not available headroom. [R1, R3]
- [`biomes/terrain.ts`](../../src/render/biomes/terrain.ts) and [`tessellate.ts`](../../src/render/biomes/tessellate.ts) bake fine/coarse heightfield art with shared borders. The current hole is one rectangle for legacy terrain, not arbitrary cave portals. [`bake-terrain.mjs`](../../scripts/bake-terrain.mjs) bundles source, hashes dependencies, runs worker-thread bakes and verifies raw recipe bytes. [`terrainCodec.ts`](../../src/render/biomes/terrainCodec.ts) already stores arbitrary triangle lists, full-sphere normals and integer cel alpha, provided vertices lie within their column and supported Y range. Its format does not require a heightfield. [R2]
- [`terrainAssets.ts`](../../src/render/biomes/terrainAssets.ts) has a separate 48 MiB decompressed LRU and neighbor prefetch. Its file names omit `layer`; another reason to keep layer 0 initially. Include cache, bake/decode temporaries and collision data in total memory reporting, not just resident mesh buffers.
- [`materials.ts`](../../src/render/materials.ts) computes cel illumination directly, with fixed directional bands, special refuge warmth and alpha `-1` for unlit color. **Adding a Babylon `SpotLight` alone will not light this shader.** Cave darkness, sunlight suppression and lamp response need shader work.

## Technology options

These are engineering assessments for this repository, not measured benchmarks. A **replacement volume** is an ownership/composition strategy; it can contain a layered field, voxels or authored meshes.

| Approach | Movement, LOS and navigation | Bake/LOD, WebGL2 and Babylon fit | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Layered heights / solid vertical spans** | Store ordered rock intervals per X/Z cell; a single floor+ceiling pair handles one gallery, multiple spans handle stacked spaces. Sweep the whole diver/hull footprint against occupied spans and their lateral faces; an endpoint height test misses ceiling edges. LOS traverses cells/spans. Navigation connects free spans in 3D; tow clearance erodes them by the actual hull envelope. | Emit top, underside and boundary-wall meshes as kit recipes. Share border spans and portal geometry at both LODs. Storage grows with horizontal samples × spans, rather than the entire volume. Lighting still requires room/occlusion metadata and shader changes. Babylon renders the resulting ordinary meshes. | Economical for terraced galleries, but shafts, branching loops and near-vertical sidewalls make span stitching and continuous collision substantially more complex than “add ceilingHeight.” |
| **SDF/voxels only around caves** | Arbitrary caves/overhangs. A point sign or density gradient is not a capsule/hull sweep. Use a collision mesh/BVH plus tested shape casts, or conservative advancement over a proven distance bound. Noise-based density is not automatically a signed distance field. LOS can traverse occupancy or query triangles. Derive a 3D clearance graph, then validate rescue routes with the same shapes. | Marching cubes is straightforward to bake; dual contouring uses Hermite samples/QEFs to retain features; `isosurface.surfaceNets` is a small JS reference implementation. Worker extraction can transfer buffers, but requires cancellation and transient-memory accounting. Mixed-resolution seams need common boundary extraction or something like Transvoxel, not a heightfield skirt. Upload triangle recipes to Babylon; no need for GPU meshing. [T1–T5] | Most geometric freedom, highest new surface area: sampling, topology, collision, navigation, meshing and seams. WebGL2 has no core compute-shader pipeline; the GPU Gems geometry-shader/stream-output implementation is not directly portable. [T2, T12] |
| **Authored mesh cave kit + mesh/convex collision** | Assemble chambers, roofs, arches, shafts and collars. Use thick box/convex **rock solids**, not one convex hull enclosing a concave cave (that seals the passage). Existing box sweeps cover a restricted first kit. General convex needs tested CCD; triangle collision needs a BVH and robust shape casts. Ray/segment queries share that shell. Author a 3D room/portal graph and swept tow polylines. | Bake and merge static rock per chunk/material; instance repeated flora. Keep mouths/throats fixed in coarse LOD. Optional offline `meshoptimizer` simplification must lock borders and preserve openings. Ordinary Babylon custom mesh upload fits the kit. Cell-local baked shading plus a lamp is sufficient initially. [T6–T9] | Least new machinery for a handful of authored caves. Art/proxy mismatch and doorway seams are the risks. More authoring work per place, much less runtime terrain machinery. |
| **Local cave replacement volumes** | Explicit domains replace base rock locally; the replacement supplies all floors, roofs and walls. Every swept actor and ray crossing a boundary must test both relevant authorities. Support selects the reachable surface below the actor, not the highest surface at X/Z. Room graphs support wildlife and rescue. | Bake the exterior patch, cave shell and their seam together, using the preceding mesh-kit approach. Keep collision resident; stream art through S1. Expand descriptor bounds below the exterior floor. Preserve visible roof/portal geometry in every LOD. Lighting uses the same room metadata, with transition collars. | Recommended composition with authored meshes. A naked “disable the heightfield inside this trigger” creates holes, rooftop leaks and seam exploits. Ownership must cover the entire replacement domain. |

**Collision library fallback:** Rapier's JS/WASM `World.castShape` supports translational shape sweeps; its fixed trimesh and convex colliders are relevant if a broader collision backend is later justified. A trimesh is a zero-thickness boundary and is not a robust point-containment oracle. Keep occupancy explicit. Babylon's built-in camera/mesh collision uses ellipsoids; enabling it would not automatically replace simulation, tow or targeting queries. Use either backend only behind the same pure world-query contract. [T7, T10]

**Cost scale, calculated rather than measured:** a 128³ m volume at 1 m spacing has `129³` float samples, about **8.2 MiB** for density alone; at 0.5 m, `257³` is **64.8 MiB**. Gradients, mesh output, BVH and worker staging add to that. Local 32 m volumes greatly reduce this cost. A mesh with 10,000 vertices and 20,000 triangles costs about **0.50 MiB** in today's float position/normal/RGBA plus 16-bit indices, before staging and a retained CPU/GPU copy. Mesh split parts can add draws. Merge rock, use chunk-local thin-instance batches for detail, avoid transparent shell layers, and measure actual visible draw count; thin instances cull as a batch. [T8, T11]

**Lighting applies to all four options.** Lower directional/ambient illumination inside rock-enclosed rooms, retain readable emissive landmarks, and implement a diver/hull lamp cone and range in the cel shader. Compose with the approved lamp-switch work when available; decorative lamps in this checkout do not establish a working handheld beam. Bake local occlusion/color variation into cave vertices; unlit flora is visually emissive but does not illuminate neighbors. Multiply both lit color and fog's inscattering contribution appropriately: an outdoor-bright fog color can wash out a dark cave. Use room-local light masks/portal visibility so sunlight and the lamp do not light through rock; a cheap unoccluded cone can otherwise reveal adjacent rooms. Start without dynamic shadow maps, then assess a bounded shadow/occlusion solution if needed. Ordinary Babylon lights also pass through meshes unless shadows are enabled. Preserve wet fog in flooded caves; a roof grants neither breathable air nor pressure protection. [T9]

## Concrete integration sketch

### 1. One content record, explicit ownership

Add a Babylon-free `src/content/regions/caves.ts` with stable IDs, composed beside structures/sites. Conceptual fields:

```ts
interface CaveDef {
  id: string;
  region: RegionId;
  replacementFootprints: readonly Rect[]; // base-terrain ownership, not empty space
  bounds: Aabb;                         // includes exterior collar and roof
  rock: readonly Solid[];               // thick box solids in the first kit
  rooms: readonly CaveRoom[];           // 3D free-water cells, optical settings
  portals: readonly CavePortal[];       // mouth/throat geometry + allowed hulls
  surfaces: readonly CaveSurface[];     // authored mesh source, fine/coarse
  routes: readonly CaveRoute[];         // diver/hull/tether/tow waypoint chains
}
```

These are proposed types, not existing APIs. `CaveRoom` contains a free-space bound and connections; `CavePortal` includes actual width/height and approach/turn space. Overlapping membership cells join one physical cave; they do not double-apply colliders. Resource, creature and refuge records reference existing registries, rather than inventing another economy. Static geometry needs no saved vertex/voxel field; discoveries and depletion use stable IDs. Add save fields only for genuinely new persistent behavior.

For the first kit use small grid-aligned replacement footprints with a collar. Within each footprint replace the base rock column throughout the playable vertical envelope, filling everything except authored open water with thick rock slabs/boxes. Supply the exterior top as well as cave floor, ceiling and sides. Constrain placements to terrain that can meet proxy-conformance tolerances; do not stair-step a walkable island into an invisible collision mismatch. Only the **surface patch being replaced** is masked from heightfield art: the cave's projected footprint must not accidentally delete its roof. Roofs must support a diver above while admitting one below. Adjacent replacements have explicit union ownership or are rejected as ambiguous.

### 2. Physical world queries before more content

Introduce a shared terrain-query adapter, keeping `worldFloor(x,z)` as the exterior authoring function. Provide occupancy/overlap, earliest swept contact, segment obstruction and `supportBelow(position, shape)` over base terrain outside replacement footprints plus cave rock inside. Shape-straddling mouths must test both; never switch terrain off based only on the actor center or a camera trigger. Include the handover in every slide, preserving conservative exhaustion and the non-deepening initial-overlap behavior.

Route `moveCapsule`, `clearance`, LOS and support through it. Replace the old floor clamp in `hullFloor`/`moveHull` and direct reads in `step.ts`: otherwise a valid underground actor is lifted through the roof toward the exterior ground. Make static clearance check the **whole** actor, including its ceiling, not only its center-bottom floor sample. Keep cabin/carrier-local worlds explicit so a cave provider does not leak into interiors. Retain legacy ramp, raised-pad and bench-cut-wall behavior; currently their handling depends on floor-function identity.

Index thick cave solids with the existing stable broad phase. Boxes reuse existing CCD; use generously clear chambers and explicit steps/bays first. If convex wedges become necessary, add a tagged shape and tested continuous box–convex SAT (face and edge-cross axes) or a vetted shape-cast backend; inflating just convex face planes is conservative and can falsely close corners. Do not pretend mesh bounding boxes are precise cave colliders.

Use an authored **3D room/portal graph**, not a sea-floor navmesh, for cave wildlife. Hull classes filter edges by swept envelope; waypoint following still sweeps every segment. A 2D Recast-style walking mesh alone cannot represent freely swimming routes. Rescue plans need shallow-accessible staging, Bobbin ingress, reachable attachment, and a validated disabled-hull tow back to repair. A diver-sized exit is not a hull rescue route. Where towing cannot work, author in-place salvage returning every critical module/material required to rebuild, as the expedition contract already permits. Update S2's access/drape checks to use these same physical queries. [R4]

### 3. Bake and stream the complete patch

Extend terrain generation to clip **multiple replacement surface polygons/rectangles**, in addition to the legacy preserve. Generate clipped boundaries explicitly rather than deleting triangles by centroid or using a skirt across a traversable mouth. Bake replacement exterior, inside shell, collision proxies and immutable portal borders from one source. Both fine and coarse recipes retain all traversal openings and light-blocking roofs; coarse geometry cannot bridge a tunnel. Quantize shared seam coordinates once in world space. Compare decoded art against collision, not merely two versions of the same tessellator.

Initially put shell meshes in each affected layer-0 recipe through `liveTerrain`/the existing bake targets. This reuses the terrain codec, source hash, compressed files, store, missing-file live fallback and raw-byte verification. Clip meshes to columns before encoding: the codec clamps X/Z, so an uncut cross-column mesh is corrupted. Include cave source in the bake dependency graph; do not let a stale asset show old portals against new physics. All cave factories must yield on `due()`; a later worker path transfers `ArrayBuffer`s and accounts for ownership/cancellation. [T5]

Expand chunk **and region** Y bounds from cave content, not only exterior `worldFloor` samples. Pin/prefetch the player's room, entry collar and next connected chunks, and expose a local readiness criterion before admitting travel into missing art. Existing swaps retain old LODs, but first admission can still leave a room undrawn under budget pressure: do not allow an invisible colliding wall. For this small first set, keep coarse cave/collar art resident while inside; account it through S1 rather than an unbounded side cache. Physical collision, navigation and rescue data never unload with art. Fit the combined render budget and separately report the decompressed cache, collision and worker peaks. [R1–R3]

## Six proposed places

All are new, story-neutral proposals tied to existing geography, not ratified placements. Depths/lengths are authoring targets requiring floor, protection, oxygen and rescue checks. Keep existing essential stock and exterior routes viable; no new critical material or clue relocation is implied. [R4]

| Place / existing region | Intended play and scale | Access, safety and payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fanvault loop — Hearth Reef** | A 20–40 m-deep limestone-like arch and 20–30 m swim loop under the west fan terrace, outside the legacy preserve. **Orientation, shelter, membrane catch pocket.** | Two visible mouths and a central skylight; diver only, with nearby craft parking. Teach roof contact and recognize the exit from inside. Shelter means current/predator cover, not free air. |
| **Splitstack undercroft — Splitspine Isles** | Extend a side of the existing stack cleft into a roofed tidal gallery, roughly 5–25 m deep and 50–70 m long. **Small-craft shortcut, glass-bearing surf ledges.** | Preserve the existing open cleft/ramp. Provide two clear mouths and a turning bay, with the carrier outside. A separate dry headland alcove can follow only after real waterline/walking support is validated. |
| **Lantern-root hall — Lantern Canopy** | A rib-side chamber at 110–145 m, with open upper approach and a short lower diver branch. **Creature den, cured resin/crust, quiet alternate route.** | Warnings at the den, a lit return rib and reliable disengagement. Keep any refuge separate from the den; preserve existing predator/refuge exclusion margins. Collect shed material without killing wildlife. |
| **Dish-lee vault — Pale Basin** | A 250–290 m chamber beneath the outer dish ridge, roughly 40–60 m across, reached through a visible eroded cliff mouth. **Silt shelter, salvage workspace, landmark.** | Park a work-sub in a broad chamber; a diver-only side alcove is optional. A dish-rim silhouette and restrained lamps identify the return. Bobbin attachment and a tow turning bay are authored before adding loss-capable work. |
| **Cool-seam gallery — Ember Rift → Mirror Divide** | A roofed alternate segment beside the existing cooled-flank connection, about 320–410 m deep and 100–140 m long. **Inter-biome shortcut, optional ceramic then rinsed-lattice niches.** | Two broad mouths, intermediate overlook/turning bay and a clear divide between cool rock and hazardous fissures. First core/ceramic/lattice stays on existing unprotected shelves; the full connection requires the service tier. Continuous tow and hazard checks cover both regions. |
| **Terrace window gallery — Meridian** | A 530–610 m-deep lateral cliff gallery with a daylight-facing window and one short descending room. **Story-neutral survey landmark, protected work pocket.** | Keep it separate from established interior/story boundaries. The carrier stages at the existing shelf; adequately rated small craft or a suited diver enters. No implied air supply. Offer a broad towable mouth or a complete in-place salvage outcome. |

For small-craft mouths, start around **10–12 m clear width and 5–6 m height**; work-sub halls around **12–16 m width and 7–9 m height**, then validate against actual `HULL_COLLIDERS`, visible hull geometry, exit poses and tow envelopes. These are initial clearance targets, not permission to overwrite a larger collider or omit turn tests. Make diver-only restrictions legible before commitment. No first-iteration cave needs to admit Hearthline.

## Tests and phased estimate

Estimates are **focused engineer-days for one implementation owner**, including targeted fixes; not a scheduling promise or measured agent throughput. Content/presentation work and independent review may overlap only with clear ownership. A prototype succeeding in one chamber does not accept the feature.

| Phase | Outcome / required checks | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0. Compose and freeze the physical contract** | Audit runtime/worldFloor/solids wiring; preserve the opening and local interiors. Specify replacement seams, support semantics and cold-load readiness. | **1–2 days** |
| **1. One actual underground loop** | A Reef-sized cave with top surface, roof, cliff mouth and diver loop; box collision, support-aware movement, LOS, bake and lamp/darkness. Test floor and roof at the same X/Z, mouth-straddling shapes, high-speed wall/ceiling sweeps, tangential slides, overlap escape and blocked targeting through rock. | **3–5 days** |
| **2. Streamed, recoverable craft cave** | One cross-chunk work-sub chamber; both LODs and decoded collision conformance; cold entry/continue, eviction/re-entry, missing/stale asset, cancellation, context loss, constrained memory. Drive in/out, block a vehicle exit, disable it, tow or salvage, save/reload inside. | **3–5 days** |
| **3. Author the remaining places** | Finish all six, update source habitats/room graphs/rescue routes and test predecessor-tier access and return margins. Fresh-player exit recognition and handling; one ordinary-control adverse run per distinct risk. | **4–7 days** |
| **4. Integrated acceptance** | Focused fast/heavy checks, production bake verification/build, representative-GPU traversal/memory measurements, fresh Sol 6.1 xhigh review and coordinator inspection per the current lean gate. | **2–3 days** |

**Total: 13–22 engineer-days**, with a useful diver cave after phases 0–1 (4–7 days). If the first kit requires general convex CCD, allow **2–4 extra days** for implementation and adversarial collision cases before scaling content; a voxel/BVH backend needs a separate spike/estimate.

Fast tests should compare indexed and full-scan answers, ensure unloaded art changes no physical query, exercise over/under-roof support, and reject a sealed/too-small rescue route. Keep the reef-rib trap and legacy pad/bench regressions. Add a synthetic low roof, a solid cube at the mouth and a blocked tow bend as negative controls. Validate both fleet strategies and guaranteed external first-material stock, with the contract's intended 25% return reserve verified through ordinary play. Heavy checks compare every fine/coarse seam pairing and independent art/collision probes, enforce accounted bytes through constrained admission and repeated travel, and verify bake/live equality. Browser acceptance includes lamp-off retreat and low-visibility vehicle return at declared hardware/settings; no GPU performance or human enjoyment is established by this research. [R3, R4]
