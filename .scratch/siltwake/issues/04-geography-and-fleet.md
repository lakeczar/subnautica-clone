## Question

What progression of geographically meaningful resources, blueprints, vehicle roles and reachable routes creates satisfying expeditions across ocean biomes, islands and research settlements? Map each material to recognizable source conditions and meaningful uses, preserve earlier-region relevance, and define distinct scout, utility and inhabited-vessel capabilities. Probe circular prerequisites, dominant vehicles, repetitive hauling, safe retreat and recovery before setting recipe costs.

## Fleet, survival and recovery interview

The story direction now delegates the hidden truth to the narrative team and confirms regional leads. The next integrated questionnaire asks about:

1. Fleet depth: specialist scout/work-sub/large walkable carrier-home (recommended); two deeply customizable hulls; or a broader fleet with simpler interiors.
2. Survival pressure: oxygen, pressure, power and repair with food/drink benefits rather than starvation (recommended); classic food/water upkeep; or gentle exploration.
3. Failed expeditions: preserved discoveries/blueprints plus recoverable cargo and repair/salvage (recommended); checkpoint reset; or substantial rebuilding after losses.

These are proposals, not selected requirements. Their answers determine expedition capacity, resource demand, route safety and how expensive vehicles can remain worth risking. Do not freeze recipes or the biome progression graph until these constraints are known.

## Confirmed constraints

The owner answered:

- Fleet: "Broader fleet, something like the cyclops to have vehicles be stored or deployed from a big one , think creatively in this one and make sure their relevant to the gameplay, resources and environment". Author an original carrier/mobile home and distinct deployable specialist craft. The familiar reference describes a role, not permission to copy a named design. The owner delegates creative hull and role choices to the design team.
- Survival: "Classic, but maybe not need water as a resource meter . Just food". Include food needs alongside oxygen, pressure, power and repairs; omit thirst and compulsory drinking-water upkeep.
- Failure: "Recovery expedition: keep discoveries and blueprints; retrieve some dropped cargo and repair or salvage a disabled vessel." Preserve knowledge, make retreat meaningful and make recovery reachable with available equipment.

Two Astra specialists are mapping these choices into fleet and world documents. Hull names, route depths, resource quantities and encounter tuning are authored proposals until reconciled; their existence is not implementation or playtest evidence. All specialist craft need a practical habitat/mission niche, and the main story must not require building every optional hull.

The current integrated questionnaire asks whether wildlife encounters emphasize evasion/deterrence, mixed hunting or armed combat, and what class of computer to target. No answer is inferred from the recommended options. Hardware belongs to the delivery decision; encounter style affects route and vessel design here.

## Answer

The owner subsequently selected **"Evade and deter: learn behavior, manage noise/light, use decoys and defensive equipment; hunting is not the main progression loop."** The dedicated-GPU answer is recorded in the delivery decision.

The authored plan now connects [seven regions and eight material families](../../../docs/WORLD_PROGRESSION.md) with [six distinct craft, a carrier and recoverable survival](../../../docs/FLEET_AND_SURVIVAL.md). The coordinator's [expedition contract](../../../docs/EXPEDITION_INTEGRATION.md) reconciles the pressure/refit ladder, personal suit access, first-source shelves, two main fleet strategies, story IDs, carrier staging and anti-softlock requirements. The carrier can be earned during the main campaign; optional craft remain useful without all being mandatory.

These documents are the concrete design baseline under the owner's invitation to think creatively, ready for review with the complete build input. Exact recipe quantities and source yields must be authored together and validated in the first implementation milestones; they are not settled through unsupported playtime estimates. Route scale, hunger timing, pressure values, six-hull usefulness and the ten-hour target require actual playtesting. The plan does not claim those systems exist yet.
